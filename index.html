01 / Why we’re building this

You had work to ship.

Instead, you’re refreshing a status page. A blocked review. A stalled build. Another interruption you can’t do anything about.

In the GitHub migration discussion, developers describe broken workflows and the cost of moving when they’re already busy. Others point out that self-hosting brings its own operational work. Both are reasons to build something better together.

Forkalope’s answer is a familiar Git workflow on software you can run, with a network of people who help operate it. More control over where your code lives, who runs it, and how you recover.

02 / The network

Our servers.
Our cloud.
Each other’s back.

A node should come with neighbors. We’re designing a network where approved operators keep replicas and reserve room to help one another recover.

Forkalope operates some nodes. Independent Sysops operate others on infrastructure they rent themselves. Co-Sysops help fund the capacity that makes the network possible.

  1. 01
    Prepare before anything breaks

    Pair approved nodes, keep authorized data replicas, and reserve recovery capacity across separate failure domains.

  2. 02
    Have a recovery partner ready

    When a node fails, its partner and operators would use the prepared replica to restore service. Recovery must preserve permissions and prevent conflicting writes.

  3. 03
    Prove it with recovery drills

    Test restores and failover, measure interruption and possible data loss, and publish the results before making availability promises.

The direction we’re building toward—not a live failover service. Replicas, routing, authentication, and spare capacity all need to work together. Shared providers can still fail, and private data belongs only on approved nodes.

06 / Questions people ask first

Start with the
hard questions.

What is Forkalope?

Forkalope is an open-source, GitHub-shaped code forge. The long-term idea is a cloud of independently operated nodes that can share authorized replicas and help one another recover when a primary home is unavailable.

Is this just another Forgejo or Gitea host?

Those projects prove that self-hosted Git works. Forkalope is exploring the layer they do not make central: portable identity and collaboration data, a visible network of operators, and recovery between approved homes. The software still has to earn that distinction.

Can I run Forkalope today?

The source and product direction are public, but the complete forge and shared recovery network are still in development. The current public build is this site. Follow the source repository for what becomes runnable and when.

Does every node become part of production?

No. The planned network separates community nodes, verified or anchor nodes, and Forkalope-operated core nodes. A hobby node can be useful for mirrors or experiments without becoming a dependency for a business customer.

What happens when a node crashes?

An approved recovery partner would use an authorized replica and reserved capacity to restore service, then the original operator would repair or rebuild the failed node. Recovery needs consistent repository data, permissions, routing, and authentication; it is a capability we are designing and testing, not a live uptime guarantee.

What does a Co-Sysop membership pay for?

A Tine is a proposed $30 monthly unit that helps fund shared servers, storage, backups, bandwidth, monitoring, and operations. It gives you a real stewardship relationship to a logical node while Forkalope or an approved operator keeps the administrative keys.

What is different about $30, $60, and $90?

One Tine associates you with a node. Two Tines support more capacity and give earlier access to network-building tools. Three Tines make you eligible to become the primary named steward of a logical node that can move between machines. None of the tiers buys a physical server or equity.

What is an Independent Sysop?

An Independent Sysop rents infrastructure in their own name, runs Forkalope, and takes responsibility for security, updates, capacity, and incidents. An approved Sysop can coordinate Co-Sysops and participate in recovery under a separate operator agreement.

Why would a business switch?

A business can choose managed hosting, dedicated capacity, or management on infrastructure it controls. The goal is familiar Git hosting without a seat tax, plus a clear path to export data and take over operations instead of being trapped by the provider.
