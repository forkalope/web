Software has no shortage of tutorials. What it lacks is a place to practice the whole job: taking responsibility for a service, understanding how it fails, communicating during an incident, and leaving it healthier than you found it.

Forkalope is being built as that place. The school is the first product. The open-source forge is the training aircraft: a real system students can inspect, change, break safely, recover, and eventually help operate as Forkalope Operators.

The short versionGround school teaches the systems. Labs build the reflexes. Supervised flight hours build judgment. A final checkride makes the work visible.

The premise

Learn to operate, not just complete lessons.

A course can show you how DNS, queues, backups, deployments, or access controls work. The job begins when several of them fail at once, the documentation is incomplete, and somebody needs a calm answer.

That kind of judgment is learned through practice. Pilots use ground school, simulators, supervised hours, checklists, and checkrides because knowing the material is different from being responsible for the outcome. SREs and DevOps engineers deserve a training path with the same seriousness.

  • Real systems, bounded risk. Work inside an environment where mistakes become lessons instead of customer emergencies.
  • Repetition, not theater. Diagnose failures, restore service, review what happened, then run the drill again.
  • Observed judgment. Mentors review how you investigated, communicated, changed the system, and verified recovery.
  • Evidence over claims. Graduate with runbooks, incident reports, patches, and checkride results that show how you work.

Fit

For people ready to be trusted with the system.

The first program is for developers, systems administrators, support engineers, and serious self-taught builders who know some fundamentals and want structured operating experience.

Good fitYou can read code and use a terminal.

You do not need years of infrastructure experience. You do need the patience to investigate, document, ask for review, and try again.

Not the starting pointThis is not a learn-to-code bootcamp.

If variables, Git, command-line tools, and basic networking are all new, a foundations course should come first.

It is also not a capture-the-flag contest, an exam-cram course, or a way to disguise unpaid customer work as education. The school should reward safe reasoning and clear communication, not speed for its own sake.

The program

Ground school. Simulator. Flight hours. Checkride.

This is hands-on SRE and DevOps training. Each phase adds responsibility only after the previous one is understood. Students can repeat drills, compare approaches, and see exactly what “ready” means before moving forward.

  1. 01
    Ground school

    Short, practical instruction on systems, networks, storage, security, observability, change management, and incident response. Every concept ends in a decision you will later make in a lab.

  2. 02
    Simulator

    Purpose-built scenarios introduce known failures: a bad deploy, an expired certificate, a full disk, a lost secret, a slow dependency, a broken restore. You can pause, inspect, reset, and try again.

  3. 03
    Supervised flight hours

    During training, work in isolated labs and designated school training environments—not customer production—with a written plan and mentor review. After passing the practical assessment, graduates may apply for separately authorized, supervised operational roles as those opportunities become available.

  4. 04
    Checkride

    Handle an unfamiliar scenario from first signal through recovery and follow-up. The standard includes technical execution, safety, communication, and the quality of the record you leave behind.

First decision

What do you check before changing anything?

What the student submits
  1. InvestigationEvidence, working theory, and the next safe check.
  2. RecoveryThe smallest reversible change that restores pushes.
  3. RecordA timeline, customer update, verification, and prevention recommendation.
Reviewer feedback“You found the broken edge connection quickly. Next time, state the blast radius before restarting anything.”

The useful lesson is not that someone memorized a command. It is that they can make a safe decision, explain it, and improve the system afterward.

Curriculum

The work around the code.

The exact sequence will evolve with the first cohorts. These are the capabilities the school is being designed to teach and test.

01Systems

Processes, filesystems, permissions, resource limits, service managers, and the path from source to a running service.

02Networks

DNS, TLS, HTTP, ports, proxies, timeouts, and how to trace a request across boundaries.

03Observability

Logs, metrics, traces, alerts, useful dashboards, and the difference between a symptom and a cause.

04Safe change

Small releases, reviews, migrations, feature flags, rollbacks, maintenance windows, and verification.

05Recovery and resilience

Backups, restores, replication, secrets, dependency failures, recovery objectives, and drills that prove the plan.

06Incident leadership

Triage, escalation, incident command, status updates, post-incident learning, runbooks, and customer judgment.

A typical labThe service is slow. The graph is loud. The first theory is wrong.

Your job is not to guess faster. It is to narrow the problem, protect the system, communicate what is known, make the smallest safe intervention, and prove that recovery held.

Evaluation

The checkride measures SRE judgment.

A working service at the end is necessary, but it is not the whole result. A lucky command is not the same as sound operation. Review is based on a published rubric.

Safety

Did you understand the blast radius, protect data, preserve a way back, and ask for help when the risk changed?

Diagnosis

Did you separate evidence from assumptions and update the working theory as new signals appeared?

Execution

Were changes small, observable, reversible, and checked against the intended result?

Communication

Could another operator and an affected customer understand the state of the incident without translating jargon?

Follow-through

Did you verify recovery, document the work, and improve the system so the next response is better?

The initial program will issue a Forkalope completion record, not an accredited degree or government license. The value has to come from the quality and inspectability of the work.

Outcomes

Qualify as a Forkalope Operator.

The goal is a compact, reviewable body of work that gives an SRE hiring manager or experienced operator something real to inspect.

  • RunbooksInstructions another person can actually use under pressure.
  • Incident reportsClear timelines, decisions, contributing conditions, and follow-up work.
  • Reviewed changesPatches and operational improvements with plans, review, and verification.
  • Drill recordScenarios attempted, feedback received, and demonstrated improvement over time.
  • CheckrideA final observed exercise scored against the published operating rubric.

Completion does not guarantee a job. It should make a graduate’s readiness for SRE and DevOps work easier to judge and harder to fake.

The network

SREs train here. Forkalope Operators power the network.

Forkalope’s longer-term advantage is not a library of videos. It is a growing group of SRE-minded people trained on the same operating principles, familiar with the same systems, and able to review one another’s work.

01Students practice on real systems.
02Graduates become trusted Forkalope Operators.
03Operators strengthen the network and mentor the next cohort.
04Customer work creates better scenarios, tools, and opportunities.

Graduation can be the beginning of a relationship, not the end of a course. Qualified graduates may contribute to the forge, help run drills, support later students, or apply for paid customer and infrastructure work as those programs open. The training should still be worth buying even if a graduate never works for Forkalope.

Network participation and paid work are separate decisions with their own review, responsibilities, and agreements. Tuition never buys a job, production access, or customer data.

Common questions

The honest version.

Is Forkalope still building a code forge?

Yes, but the forge is no longer the whole pitch. It is the open-source system students learn on and Forkalope Operators can eventually help run. The school gives the software a focused first customer and gives the future network a way to develop trusted people.

Is this SRE or DevOps training?

Primarily SRE: reliability, production systems, observability, incidents, recovery, and responsible change. DevOps is useful adjacent language because the work also touches delivery, automation, and collaboration across development and operations.

Is this a coding bootcamp?

No. The program begins after basic coding, Git, and command-line foundations. It focuses on operating software safely: diagnosis, change, recovery, security, communication, and accountability.

Will I work on real customer systems?

During training, students work in isolated labs and designated school training environments—not customer production. After passing the practical assessment, graduates may apply for separately authorized, supervised operational roles as those opportunities become available. Graduation alone does not grant production access or employment.

Does graduation guarantee paid work?

No. The goal is to create credible evidence and a path into a trusted network. Hiring, contracting, and operator access remain separate decisions.

When does the first cohort start?

The date, format, schedule, tuition, and prerequisites are still being designed. People who register interest will help shape those decisions and hear when the founding cohort is ready.
