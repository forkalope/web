The ground rules / Terms

Build together.
Know the deal.

The proposed ground rules for supporting Forkalope, operating a node, and buying services from the network.

Draft updated September 13, 2026 · Effective date not yet set

Working draft · not an active service agreement

These are starting terms for services we are designing. Reading this page or choosing a preview plan does not sign you up, authorize a charge, or create a payout entitlement. Final terms, the legal contracting party, and a contact address must be supplied before enrollment.

1. Software and services

Forkalope’s open-source software is governed by the license supplied with its source. Hosted services, memberships, and paid work would have separate agreements. Running the software yourself does not require a paid membership.

Before a paid service begins, its order must identify the provider, included features and limits, price, and applicable terms. Business service or data-processing agreements may add specific commitments. Planned features on this site are not a promise that those features are available today.

2. Co-Sysops and Tines

The proposed membership levels are $30, $60, and $90 per month, representing one, two, or three Tines of infrastructure support. Tines help fund shared capacity, backups, monitoring, and operations. They do not purchase a particular physical machine.

Co-Sysops would receive the stewardship features listed in their final plan. Node affiliation and approved naming privileges do not confer legal ownership, root access, access to customer data, or authority to bind Forkalope. Hardware and node assignments may change as the network is maintained.

Dues do not buy equity, a financial return, or a share of node revenue. Contributing more or joining earlier does not increase commission rates. Community participation is not legal cooperative membership or corporate voting power unless a separate agreement expressly establishes it.

3. Independent Sysops

An Independent Sysop would rent infrastructure in their own name, remain responsible for their provider’s contract and charges, and administer their own machines. They could coordinate Co-Sysops affiliated with an approved network node.

Joining the shared hosting or recovery network would require an operator agreement covering security, updates, backups, reserved capacity, incident response, customer data, abuse handling, and exit or migration procedures. Merely installing Forkalope would not authorize someone to host network customers or receive their private replicas.

Node sponsorship allocations and compensation for delivered hosting would follow that agreement. Coordinating members would not create an upline or a right to their future earnings. Forkalope would not automatically reimburse a Sysop’s server bill or guarantee customers.

4. Earn through customers and work

A separate program may compensate qualified participants for directly sourced customers, migrations, support, approved bounties, or contracted infrastructure service. No one earns a recruitment commission for bringing in Co-Sysops or Sysops, or a percentage of another participant’s earnings.

The proposed principle is equal published rules, with no founder multipliers or membership-tier advantages. Paid membership would not be required to apply for customer or work opportunities.

Rates, eligibility, attribution, acceptance criteria, refund adjustments, taxes, payment timing, and the duration of recurring commissions must be specified in separate program terms before work is commissioned. Illustrative earnings are not an offer or guarantee of income.

5. Your content and responsible use

You retain your rights in your code and content. A hosted service agreement would grant only the permissions needed to store, process, display, back up, and replicate it to deliver the service you select. Public content remains subject to the licenses and permissions its owners choose.

Users must have authority to upload their content, protect their credentials, and respect other people’s rights. Unauthorized access, attacks, unlawful content, harassment, and abuse of shared resources would be prohibited. Private customer data would not be made available to sponsors simply because they support the node.

6. Shared recovery and availability

We are designing a network in which approved operators can keep authorized replicas and reserve capacity to help one another recover. This is a development direction, not a current guarantee of automatic or instantaneous failover, uninterrupted service, or zero data loss.

Actual recovery depends on the selected service, replica freshness, spare capacity, and functioning dependencies. Customers should keep appropriate independent backups. Any recovery targets, uptime commitment, support response, or service credit must appear in the applicable service agreement.

7. Billing, cancellation, and leaving

Before checkout is enabled, it must state the charge and currency, billing interval, any automatic renewal, taxes, cancellation method, and refund policy. The intended model is monthly membership with a way to stop future renewals. No payment is taken through the current preview.

Final terms must explain when cancellation takes effect, what happens to node affiliation, and how data can be exported before a hosted account closes. Refund eligibility, backup expiration, notice of price changes, and operator offboarding remain to be finalized. Applicable mandatory consumer rights are not waived by these draft terms.

8. Safety, changes, and disputes

Service terms should allow proportionate restrictions when necessary to address abuse, security threats, nonpayment, or legal obligations, with notice and a review route where feasible. The final agreement must explain termination, data access, warranties, liability, dispute handling, and any governing law.

We will publish a dated version before asking anyone to accept terms and explain material changes. See the draft privacy policy for the proposed approach to personal information and independent nodes.

Still to complete before launch

Legal entity and working contact; final plan benefits; checkout and cancellation terms; refund rules; acceptable-use and reporting procedures; operator and partner agreements; business service limits; and legal review for the regions where we offer services.

This draft intentionally leaves those decisions open. It is not a complete agreement for taking payments or hosting customer data.
